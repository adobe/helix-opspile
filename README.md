# helix-opspile

An inbox for opsophiles: one place for an ops team to work through every Slack, Microsoft
Teams and Discord channel it is responsible for, with per-person and per-team read state, and
a [customer portal](#customer-portal) for the customers who can use none of the three.

Status: **all four piles live.** Signed Slack events are stored in R2 and queued for
normalization; Teams: a cron keeps one tenant-wide Graph subscription alive, and its
notifications and the messages they name are stored the same way; Discord: a Durable Object
polls the servers it reads every minute and stores their new messages, and every few minutes
the last day's edits, deletions and reactions; the customer portal, a second Worker, delivers
what happens in it as events (pile 1). A queue consumer
turns every raw object into one markdown file per message (pile 2), filed by the channel rules
in D1, and labels, re-threads and titles the threads with Workers AI. The inbox (pile 4) shows
them per team with read state, status piles, filters, replies, reactions, ✅ to resolve and
shared drafts, all as the person signed in; the admin pages manage teams, members, rules,
customers and which bots are Adobe's, and each team's and customer's agent: its thread agents, tools, MCP
servers, instructions and knowledge base (pile 3). Thread agents draft
replies and fill the knowledge bases in the channels where they are switched on. Agents outside
the browser work the inbox through `/mcp`, and agents in the browser through WebMCP. See
[docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) for the design and the open issues for what
comes next.

## Ingest Worker

One Cloudflare Worker, deployed as `helix-opspile-prod` and `helix-opspile-stage`. Production
answers on its custom domain (`WORKER_DOMAINS`, the `PUBLIC_ORIGIN`); its `workers.dev` address
stays on as a retired origin (`RETIRED_ORIGINS`): pages there redirect, and machine endpoints
still answer.

### Inbox and Access

`/inbox`, `/api` and `/spectrum` (with `/inbox/admin` under them) are one
Cloudflare Access application. People sign in with **Slack** (Slack's OpenID Connect, through the opspile
Slack app), **Microsoft** (Entra ID, the opspile app registration, our tenant only), a
**one-time code** by email, or a **passkey**. Whichever they use, the application's allow policy decides, by
email: it lists the staff members of the ops teams, and the Worker keeps it so (see
`ACCESS_APP_ID` above) after every membership change in the admin pages and on the cron. The
Worker trusts only a verified `Cf-Access-Jwt-Assertion` whose email is a person in D1 on an ops
team, and shows them the threads of their own teams.

**Passkeys** are Access's own login method (the account's *Passkeys* identity provider, allowed
in the application's login methods; the login page does not open the browser's passkey prompt by
itself). Anyone the policy lets in may add one: on the Access login page, *Sign in with a
passkey*, then *Don't have a passkey? Set one up*, and sign in once with Slack, Microsoft or a
code, which ties the passkey to that email. After that the passkey alone signs them in, to
the same email, so the policy and the Worker see no difference. *Manage passkeys* on the same
page renames or deletes one; an admin removes a lost one under Zero Trust, *Users*. A passkey
lets nobody in by itself: someone the policy drops (off every team) is out, passkey or not.

**No-team mode.** With `SIGNIN_DOMAINS`, anyone with an email at one of those domains may sign
in too, team or not (the policy gets those domains, see `SIGNIN_DOMAINS` above). Someone on no
team becomes a person in D1 when they first sign in, if they are not one yet (staff if the
domain is a staff domain), and has only the personal view: the channels they posted or were
mentioned in, and what they follow (products, topics, customers; the inbox suggests some, see
`GET /api/follows/suggest`). The MCP server's `list_threads` and `search_threads` are of that view
for them. Without `SIGNIN_DOMAINS`, only team members get in.

**The personal view** is everyone's, on a team or not: the conversations in the channels one
posted or was mentioned in, whichever team they are filed under. Anyone reads, replies, reacts,
resolves, drafts and seeds agents in those threads, as well as in their teams' inboxes. The inbox
listens to the rooms of the teams those channels are filed under (`/api/me`'s `rooms`); in a
room of a team one is not on, the socket is *scoped* (`src/room.js`): it hears only about the
threads of the channels of one's personal view (`recipients` in `src/shares.js`).
At the end of that view's unread list, once every page is loaded and no customer or platform
filter is narrowing it, the page names the teams that still have unread threads the view does
not already show. Fewer members come first, and the admin team last. A team's name opens its
inbox with unread still on, and without the type filter, so an alert in the count is on screen.

Access returns to the requested path after a Slack or Microsoft login, but without its query
string; pages that need parameters across a sign-in carry them in the path (see the OAuth
consent page below). Both identity providers redirect to
`https://<team>.cloudflareaccess.com/cdn-cgi/access/callback`, which must be listed in the
Slack app's redirect URLs and the Entra app's web redirect URIs.

### Spectrum 2

The pages are in Adobe's [Spectrum 2](https://s2.spectrum.adobe.com) design. Each `ui/<page>.html`
(`<html class="spectrum">`) has no styles of its own; it links Spectrum 2's typeface, Adobe
Clean Spectrum VF (`ui/spectrum-fonts.css`, from Adobe Fonts); `spectrum-tokens.css`, which
`src/page.js` builds from the Spectrum 2 design tokens the page's stylesheets use
(`@spectrum-css/tokens`, light and dark, with the large scale on touch screens);
`ui/spectrum.css` (what every page shares: the base, the header, the colors the pages name, the
controls); and `ui/spectrum-<page>.css` (the page's own: its layout, then its look). Those
stylesheets set colors, sizes, spaces, radii and durations with tokens only
(`test/ui-spectrum.test.js`): spaces from the spacing scale (a value between two steps as their
sum, an odd pixel with the hairline border width), the layout's larger sizes on its 8px grid
plus the rest (`calc(var(--spectrum-spacing-100) * n + …)`), text on the type ramp, radii,
borders and durations on theirs. A zero length keeps its unit (`0px`): calc() cannot add a
unitless 0 to a length. Icons a stylesheet draws (a chevron, a checkmark, a cross) are
Spectrum 2's workflow icons as masks, in the color of the text: an empty
`<span class="s2-chevron" aria-hidden="true">` (`right` for a closed disclosure), `s2-check`,
`s2-cross`, not a `▾`, `✓` or `✕` in the text. A checkbox is Spectrum 2's, drawn by
`ui/spectrum.css`. Popovers and dialogs arrive as Spectrum 2's do (a popover over 190ms from
4px away, a dialog over 250ms from 20px, both gone in 130ms), and nothing moves for someone
who asked the browser for less motion. A page takes a closed dialog out with
`Dialog.remove(dlg)` (`ui/app.js`), which waits for it to have left; whoever waits for its
answer does not.

Every page is framed as Spectrum 2's [app frame](https://spectrum.adobe.com/foundations/app-frame)
is: the header and what navigates (the inbox's filters, a list of teams or knowledge bases)
sit on the frame's layer (`--frame`, background layer 1), and what is picked there is the
content area, one container on the base (`--content`), extra-large rounded, with the
emphasized drop shadow and no border. Inside it, parts that belong together are divided by a
hairline (the thread list and the open thread), sections by space and their titles; COGS and
Topics, which are dashboards, put their cards straight on the frame. Nothing else has a
shadow unless it floats (a menu, a popover, the filters slid out over the list), nothing has
a dashed edge but a drop zone and the agent's thought bubble, and a page has one
high-attention action at most: what deletes or declines is a secondary (outline) button,
red only where a dialog confirms it. Labels are in sentence case, also where they are stored
values (`vendor` shows as "Vendor"). No field has a placeholder: it has a label above it
(`<label class="fld">`, or a form's own), help text under it for a format or an example, and
a field that filters or finds has the magnifier (`<label class="s2-search">`) and an
`aria-label`. What a filter's value or a thread's kind and urgency is, the inbox shows with
Spectrum 2's workflow icons (`wf('alert-diamond')` in `ui/inbox.js`, a mask in
`ui/spectrum-inbox.css`); emoji are for what people put on messages. Deleting a team is
asked about first, in an alert dialog. The browser's chrome (`theme-color`) is the frame's
layer.
Your menu's **Appearance** picks the color scheme per browser (localStorage `opspile.scheme`):
the system's, as the browser prefers it (the default), light or dark. `ui/header.js` sets it as
`data-scheme` on `<html>`, which the tokens and the browser's chrome follow.

Code blocks in messages are highlighted with [`@pierre/highlights`](https://diffs.com/highlights),
the highlighter of slicc's chat (`src/ui-highlight.js`). A fence that names no language, as all of
Slack's, gets one by what it looks like (JSON, XML or HTML, a diff, Apache rewrites, HTTP, logs, SQL,
Java, JavaScript, Python, CSS, shell commands, YAML), and stays plain text when nothing is sure; so
does a block over 100,000 characters, and a line of 1,000 or more is one run. The highlighter writes
classes (`hl-keyword`, `hl-string`, …), not colors, and `ui/spectrum-inbox.css` colors them with
Spectrum 2's 1200 shades, which keep 4.5:1 on a code block in either color scheme
(`test/ui-highlight.test.js`); on your own bubble the code stays white. It is not in the page: the
inbox imports `/inbox/highlight.js` with the first code block it shows, which is plain text until
then.

A customer's stack is drawn as an FMC block diagram (`src/fmc.js`, after the
[FMC visualization guidelines](https://www.fmc-modeling.org/visualization_guidelines)): Graphviz
places the parts, and the renderer draws them as SVG with classes (`fmc-…`) that
`ui/spectrum-inbox.css` gives Spectrum 2 colors, so it follows the color scheme too. The inbox
imports it, `/inbox/fmc.js`, the first time it shows a stack. `npm run ui:dev` has a gallery of it
at `/__dev/fmc`: the guidelines' figures next to the same structures as DOT drawn by it, and the
fake data's stacks (`scripts/fmc-gallery.mjs`).

Until it was every page's look it was a preview under `/spectrum`; those addresses redirect to the
same pages under `/inbox`. `/spectrum` is still a path of the Access application (see "Inbox
and Access"), so they are behind it like the rest.

### MCP server

`/mcp` lets an agent outside the browser (Claude, an IDE, a CLI) work the inbox as the person
signed in, with eighteen tools: `whoami`, `list_threads`, `search_threads` (what `/api/search`
finds, in all of the person's teams, the knowledge bases' entries as `knowledge`),
`get_knowledge` (one of those entries, as markdown), `get_thread`, `get_message`, `post_draft`, `post_as_user`,
`post_as_bot`, `react_to_message`, `resolve_thread`, `seed_agent`, and the extraction rules and
tech stack of a team (`list_extraction_rules`, `test_extraction_rule`, `save_extraction_rule`,
`delete_extraction_rule`, `save_tech`, `delete_tech`; the changes for the admin team and the
team's managers), so an agent can be asked to update the rules. A thread
is answered three ways, as in the inbox: `post_draft` saves its shared draft (`PUT /api/draft`;
`base` names the version it replaces), which the team sees and sends, with the `sources` it
rests on, which only the team sees; `post_as_user` posts as
the person (`POST /api/post`); `post_as_bot` posts as the opspile app (`POST /api/post/app`),
where the channel lets the app post. Both post tools take the shared draft's version as
`draft`, which uses it up; `get_thread` shows the draft and `can_post_as_bot`.
`get_thread` returns its messages whole; past 40,000 characters together, the longest are cut
to the same length and marked `truncated`, and `get_message` returns one of them whole.

The admin team gets more tools, which nobody else sees in `tools/list` (for them it is
`cacheScope: private`) or can call (an unknown tool):

| Tool | What |
| --- | --- |
| `get_thread_trace` | Any thread like `get_thread`, with its agent's work in the same timeline, oldest first: what it was told, what it thought and said, every tool call with its arguments and result, and every MCP server call a code mode script made (`mcp_call`: arguments and status, not results; the agent keeps its latest 200); its prompt and tool changes; its model, tools, MCP servers, latest turn and token use. From the agent's transcript since its last reset; `limit`, `before` (paging) and `max_chars`. A channel without agents has no trace (its agent is not started to look). |
| `list_tech_conflicts` | The conflicts between the teams' technologies, as the admin team's inbox has them, a thread each: an id two teams define otherwise (`diverging`), or a name or alias two ids share (`name`); each with its `key`, the definitions, and `kept` when an admin kept it as it is. |
| `resolve_tech_conflict` | Settles one, by `key`, as you: `keep` (a `note`), `use` (a team's definition everywhere), `delete` (one definition), `unalias` (a shared name off one technology) or `merge` (`from` into `into`: every file of `from` goes and `into` lists it in `moved_from`, which rewrites what D1 keeps under it for good). Writes the teams' catalog files, or the D1 rows. |

It speaks MCP `2026-07-28`
over Streamable HTTP: stateless, `server/discover`, version and capabilities in each
request's `_meta`, header checks (`MCP-Protocol-Version`, `Mcp-Method`, `Mcp-Name`), caching
hints, and plain JSON responses (no SSE, no `subscriptions/listen`). Clients of the 2025
revisions get `initialize` and the same tools, without a session. `post_as_user` and
`post_as_bot` post at once: the server never asks (no elicitation, which a client can claim and
then decline on its own), so the client's tool approval is the check. `get_thread` does not
mark anything read.

Sign-in is OAuth 2.1, served by the Worker itself (`src/oauth.js`): discovery
(`/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource`, `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`), open
dynamic client registration (`/oauth/register`, any https redirect URI or localhost), and
`/oauth/token` (PKCE S256, refresh tokens rotated). There is no list of allowed clients:
`/oauth/authorize` (public) signs the request into the path of the consent page `/api/oauth/consent/…`, behind Access like the rest of `/api` (Access keeps the path through a Slack or Microsoft login but drops the query); it shows who
asks and, in large type, the host the grant goes to, and the person decides. Codes and
tokens are stored hashed in D1 (`oauth_*`, migration 0015); an access token lives an hour.
`/mcp` itself is **not** behind Access; it takes these tokens (or an Access assertion, for
`cloudflared access curl`). Connect a client to `<origin>/mcp`.

### Thread agents

An agent per thread in the channels where agents are on follows the thread, keeps its
shared draft current (a person sends it), and fills the customer's and the team's knowledge
bases (OKF; see ARCHITECTURE, "Thread agents"). To turn them on:

1. Create an AI Gateway in the Worker's account (`AGENT_GATEWAY` is its id). Leave it
   unauthenticated, or create a token and set the secret `AI_GATEWAY_TOKEN`.
2. Create an [Amazon Bedrock API key](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/api-keys.html)
   that may invoke the model's inference profile in `AGENT_REGION`, and set it as the Worker
   secret `BEDROCK_API_KEY` (`npx wrangler secret put BEDROCK_API_KEY --name helix-opspile-prod`).
3. Export `AGENT_MODEL`, `AGENT_GATEWAY` and, optionally, `AGENT_CHANNELS` (and for CI, add
   them as repository secrets), apply the migrations and deploy.
4. Turn agents on or off per channel on the team's page in Teams (**Thread agents**): the
   admin team for every channel, team managers for their teams' channels.

GitHub repositories the agents may read are rows in `agent_repos`, data like the rules:
`INSERT INTO agent_repos (scope, slug, url, note) VALUES ('org', 'acme', 'https://github.com/acme/site', 'their site')`
(`scope` is `all`, `team` or `org`). The cron mirrors them into Artifacts within 20 minutes.
`GET /diag/agent?name=<thread key>` shows what an agent is doing (and its MCP servers); the
admin team sees everything it did with the MCP tool `get_thread_trace`.

**MCP servers.** Each ops team and each customer can have MCP servers for its agents, added on
the team's or the customer's page (Teams or Customers, **Tools and MCP servers**; whoever may
edit its knowledge base): a name, the server's `https://` Streamable HTTP URL, what
it is for, its credentials, and optionally the only tools agents may call. Credentials are
either request headers (sealed with `TOKEN_KEY`, never shown again) or an **OAuth** sign-in:
**Connect** sends whoever manages the server to the server's own sign-in (opspile finds its
authorization server, signs in there with its client ID metadata document
`<PUBLIC_ORIGIN>/oauth/client.json` where the server takes one, else registers itself, unless a
client id is given, and uses PKCE), and
the agents then call the server **as that person**, with what they may do there, until someone
disconnects it. Tokens are refreshed as they expire, one refresh at a time for all agents, and
the cron refreshes grants unused for a week so that refresh tokens stay alive. For a server that
takes neither, register a client there with the redirect URI
`<PUBLIC_ORIGIN>/api/kb/mcp/oauth/callback` and give its client id (and secret). Adding a server
connects to it and lists its tools. An agent gets the servers of its thread's team and customer,
and calls them only from code mode scripts (`await tools.mcp__<name>__<tool>(args)`); see
ARCHITECTURE, "Thread agents".

opspile's own `/mcp` is such a server: added with the URL `<PUBLIC_ORIGIN>/mcp` and **OAuth**,
and connected by someone, it gives the agents what that person sees in the inbox (and their
posting tools: limit it with **Only these tools**, e.g. `list_threads, search_threads,
get_thread, get_thread_trace`).

**Global MCP servers.** Four servers are built in, set up once for every team; the browser and
GitHub are on for the teams created since they are on by default, and otherwise all are off until
the admin team or a team's managers turn them on (Slack channel management: the admin team
only), for the team (in the team's knowledge base, **MCP servers**) or for one channel
(**Per channel**; a channel's switch wins over its team's):

- **`browser`**, Cloudflare's hosted [Browser Run MCP server](https://github.com/cloudflare/mcp-server-cloudflare/tree/main/apps/browser-rendering):
  pages as markdown or HTML, screenshots, PDFs, links, elements by selector, JSON extraction,
  crawls. It needs the secret `BROWSER_RUN_TOKEN`: an account API token with *Browser Run
  Write* only.
- **`github`**, [GitHub's MCP server](https://github.com/github/github-mcp-server), read-only
  (`/mcp/readonly`, lockdown mode), as the opspile **GitHub App**: it reads exactly the
  repositories the app is installed on. To set it up, a member of the admin team opens
  **MCP servers** of any team's knowledge base, fills in the GitHub organization that should
  own the app (or nothing, for their own account) and **Create on GitHub**; GitHub asks to
  confirm, opspile keeps the app and sends them on to install it, on all of an account's
  repositories or a selection. The app is public, so it can be installed on any account
  (customers' organizations too), and so anyone can install it: an installation counts only
  once the admin team **Allow**s it in the same place (and **Revoke** takes it back).
  **Install elsewhere or change repositories** changes where it is installed. With several
  allowed installations, the server is there once per account (`github_<account>`). The app
  only asks for read access; it needs no webhook.
- **`fluffyjaws`**, FluffyJaws' MCP server (`https://api.fluffyjaws.adobe.com/api/v1/mcp`):
  Adobe's internal support assistant, which searches its knowledge, product documentation,
  Slack, the wiki, Jira, GitHub code and support tickets. Its tools but
  `content_compass_recommend` (which saves a conversation there). It takes an Okta token of its
  own authorization server (`FLUFFYJAWS_ISSUER`), in one of two ways:
  - **As a person** (`FLUFFYJAWS_CLIENT_ID`, opspile's Okta client; FluffyJaws' Okta does not
    let clients register themselves): a member of the admin team opens **MCP servers** of any
    knowledge base and **Connect as you** under FluffyJaws, and signs in at Okta. opspile keeps
    the grant sealed and refreshes it, and **every agent where FluffyJaws is on acts as that
    person** there, seeing what they see; the editor says whose identity it is, to everyone.
    **Connect again** replaces it (another person signs in), **Disconnect** revokes it. The
    daily check of the global servers refreshes the token, so the refresh token stays in use.
  - **As a service app** (`FLUFFYJAWS_SERVICE_CLIENT_ID`, `FLUFFYJAWS_SERVICE_SECRET`), which
    wins when both are set: no person, `X-FluffyJaws-End-User: automation`. To create one,
    request an Okta **Service** app (API services: client credentials, client secret) the way
    Okta apps are requested, have it allowed the scope `fluffyjaws` on FluffyJaws' authorization
    server, and register its client id in FluffyJaws' **Integrations** workspace (FluffyJaws'
    docs, "Register an app"); then set the two bindings and deploy.
- **`slack_channels`**, Slack channel management as AEM Ops Automation, from the
  slack-automation relay's `SlackMcp` entrypoint, which has no public URL: opspile reaches it
  through the service binding `SLACK_MCP` (bound with `SLACK_AUTOMATION_WORKER`), so there is
  no credential. Two tools read (`channel_profile`: who is in a channel, and how;
  `person_status`: one person's account), six write at once, without anyone confirming them:
  `invite_guest`, `add_to_channel`, `remove_from_channel`, `invite_slack_connect`,
  `disconnect_org` and `archive_channel`. The relay reports each write to the Slack admins, with
  `asked_by` and `reason`, which the agents are told to pass (the Slack user id of the person in
  the thread who asked, and why), and keeps its guards (the invite rules, no disconnection while
  leaving people have no guest account, never #general). It is for agents of Slack threads only,
  off for new teams, and **only the admin team switches it**, for a team or one Slack channel.

See ARCHITECTURE, "Global MCP servers".

**Agent tools.** Some of the agents' tools are off until the admin team or a team's managers
turn them on (a new team starts with **`computer`**, **`extraction_rules`**, **`web_search`**, **`resolve`** and **`label`** on), the same way and on the same page (**Tools and MCP servers**, group **Tools**):
for the team, or for one channel (**Per channel**; a channel's switch wins over its team's):

- **`post_message`**: the agent posts a reply in its thread right away, as the opspile bot of
  the platform, next to keeping the draft, and reacts to messages with an emoji
  (`add_reaction`). The Slack app needs the bot scopes `chat:write` and `reactions:write`
  (reinstall it after adding a scope), the Discord bot the *Send Messages*, *Send Messages
  in Threads* and *Add Reactions* permissions; Teams needs the opspile Teams bot (below),
  which cannot react: Teams lets no bot. A platform whose bot is not set up does not get the
  tool.
- **`computer`**: pi's `bash` tool, on [Cloudflare Computer](https://github.com/cloudflare/computer):
  GNU bash and real programs on the [SLICC kernel](https://github.com/ai-ecoverse/slicc-kernel),
  their processes in Dynamic Workers (the `LOADER` binding), over a working directory per
  thread that keeps its files. The shell reaches the internet without a
  filter, so what an agent read can leave through it; the prompt says not to send anything to
  sites that are not the customer's or Adobe's. **Secrets**, added in the Computer tool of a
  team's or a customer's knowledge base, are API keys the agents use without holding them: the
  shell has `$NAME` as a placeholder, and the Worker's gateway, which every request of the
  shell goes through, puts the value in only in HTTPS requests to the hosts the secret lists
  (and takes it out of the answer). A request that names one for another host is refused.
  With it the agent has **`request_bash`**: it asks a person on the team to run a command in
  that shell (a command, a reason, a timeout, a deadline, and the variables the person fills
  in, such as their own API token). The inbox shows the request under the thread, with **Run**
  and **Deny**; the agent is told later how it ended. What the person fills in goes to that one
  run and is kept nowhere, and is taken out of what the agent reads of the output; the page can
  remember it on the device, encrypted.
  **Image**, in the same row of a team's knowledge base, is the programs of that team's
  agents' computers, and only the admin team chooses it: **Base** (the default: coreutils,
  grep, ripgrep, sed, gawk, jq, yq, find, diff, tar, gzip, zip, xz, html2markdown, curl, dig,
  host, nslookup, openssl, git, `qjs`, a JavaScript interpreter, and `dot`), **Data** (Base,
  with sqlite3 and strings) or **GitHub** (Base, with `gh`, the GitHub CLI; give the team a
  `GH_TOKEN` secret for `api.github.com`).
  An image is a stack of read-only layers, as a container image is, defined in
  [scripts/computer-images.mjs](scripts/computer-images.mjs). They live in the `IMAGES`
  bucket, not in the Worker, and a computer mounts its image at `/opt/image` without copying
  it. A program's own TCP reaches port 443 of a site, past the secrets, so `openssl s_client`
  shows the site's own certificate chain: see docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, "The computer's shell".
- **`any_thread`**: the agent reads any thread of the inbox (`read_any_thread`), by its key or a
  link to one of its messages, finds a customer's channels by the customer's or a channel's name
  or the sites their threads are about (`find_channels`), and lists a channel's conversations
  (`list_any_channel_threads`). Only the admin team turns it on, one channel at a time, in
  internal channels only (the org's type is **Internal**, and the channel is not shared). See
  ARCHITECTURE, "Agent tools".
- **`membership`**: where someone in the thread asks for people to be added to the channel (or
  removed), the agent files it with the admins' bot (`request_membership`), which neither adds
  anyone itself nor lets the agent: on Teams admin-bot (teams-admin-agent), whose admins approve
  in their Slack channel; on Slack AEM Ops Automation (the slack-automation relay), which the
  agent's post in #general mentions, and whose confirmations the person who asked (a member) or
  an admin clicks. Each bot ignores what other bots post, so opspile reaches both through
  service bindings (`TEAMS_ADMIN_WORKER`, `SLACK_AUTOMATION_WORKER`); deploy those Workers with
  their `MembershipRequests` entrypoint first. A platform whose bot is not bound does not get it.
- **`web_search`**: the agent searches the public web (`web_search`): titles, addresses and
  descriptions (on Web IQ, the passages that answer the query) of up to 10 pages, which it
  reads with the `browser` MCP server or its computer. On for every team (migration 0077) and
  every new one. Each query leaves Adobe for the search provider (`WEB_SEARCH_PROVIDER`), so the
  prompt has the agent write general queries, never the customer's name, people, hosts or the
  thread's words; the Worker logs that a search ran, never its query. Two services can answer:
  - [Microsoft Web IQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/web-iq/) (`webiq`): create a Web IQ resource
    in an Azure subscription, an API key of its profile in the Web IQ portal, and set it as the
    repository secret `WEBIQ_API_KEY` (`gh secret set WEBIQ_API_KEY`): the deploy uploads it
    with each version of the Worker (`--secrets-file`), so a redeploy keeps it.
    With the key, Web IQ is the provider unless `WEB_SEARCH_PROVIDER` says otherwise. Azure
    bills it, $7 per 1,000 searches.
  - Cloudflare's [Web Search API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-search/), on the `AI`
    binding (`ceramic`, the default without a Web IQ key, `linkup`, `exa`). AI Gateway bills
    it, at the provider's list price ($0.25 per 1,000 searches for Ceramic.ai): load
    **credits** on the account, or store the provider's key on the gateway
    (`WEB_SEARCH_GATEWAY`, default `AGENT_GATEWAY`) with the alias `default`.
- **`resolve`**: the agent marks its thread resolved (`resolve_thread`) once its issue or
  request is settled, or when someone asks it to in the sidebar, and takes that back. It counts
  as a staff ✅ on the first message, in the inbox only: nothing is posted or reacted in the
  channel. A customer's next message opens the thread again, and so does the ✅ button, which
  says when only the agent resolved a thread.
- **`label`**: the agent corrects Clef's kind, urgency and next actor of its thread
  (`label_thread`), in the inbox only. The kind holds until the agent changes it, the urgency
  and next actor until a person posts again; a label review wins over the agent's.
  `resolve` and `label` are on for every team (migration 0091) and every new one.

  Where the provider cannot be paid, every search fails and the agent goes on without it.

### Rules and replay

Rows in the `rules` table map a channel to an ops team, an org and a kind: for each target,
the matching rule with the lowest `priority` wins. `field` is `channel_id`, `name`,
`group_name` (the Teams team) or `connected` (Slack Connect team ids); `pattern` is a
case-insensitive regular expression and `value` may use `$1`. For example
`org | name | ^aem-(.+)$ | $1` files `aem-acme` under the org `acme`.

A rule of target `follow` files nothing: every follow rule that matches a channel shares it with
its team (`value`), unless the channel is filed under that team, as a share by hand does
(`src/followrules.js` writes them to `channel_teams`, after every rule change and on the cron).
A follow rule may also have the field `org` (migration 0098): it matches the org the other rules
file a channel under (`^acme$`), so the team follows that customer, every channel of it, now and
later. Its managers and the admin team follow a customer, or no more, on its page in Customers
(`PUT /api/admin/org-follows`).
Where two teams' rules match the same channel, and no manual assignment decides it, the admin
team settles the conflict in its inbox, where it is a thread (`src/ruleconflicts.js`): either team files the
channels, the other rule leaves its pattern out, and the other team may follow them; or it is
kept as it is.

The admin team edits teams, members, rules, manual assignments and orgs at `/inbox/admin`,
which also re-files the channels a change moves. Rules changed in D1 by hand need a replay
with `--clear`.

Rows in `partner_domains` (`domain`, `name`) mark people from a partner's mail domain (or a
subdomain of it) as partners on their profile card, instead of customers:
`INSERT INTO partner_domains (domain, name) VALUES ('agency.example', 'Agency')`.

Pile 2 is derived, so it can always be rebuilt from pile 1:

```sh
node scripts/replay.mjs --dry-run           # count what would be replayed
node scripts/replay.mjs --prefix slack/     # replay part of pile 1
node scripts/replay.mjs                     # after raising FORMAT (src/channels.js): rewrite what renders differently
node scripts/replay.mjs --clear             # after changing rules: empty pile 2, replay all
node scripts/replay.mjs --rethread          # redo re-threading for every root (after a replay)
node scripts/replay.mjs --rethread --channel <id>   # redo one channel's, oldest root first
node scripts/replay.mjs --relabel           # label every thread again (titles, Clef, tech stack)
node scripts/replay.mjs --vectorize         # embed every channel thread and its messages again
```

The threads a channel had before the inbox did come in through the **history import**
(`src/history.js`, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, "History import"): every known Slack and Teams
channel's threads started since a day (`--since`, or `--days` back), read from
`conversations.history` and Graph's channel messages, one queue job a page. They are filed like any, but as `imported`: read by everyone,
waiting on nobody, quiet in the inbox, and without agent, label, vectors or full-text items
until `replay.mjs --relabel`, `--vectorize` or `--index` makes them. Alert channels only with
`--alerts`; Discord's poller reads `DISCORD_HISTORY_DAYS` back by itself.

```sh
node scripts/import.mjs --days 90 --dry-run       # how many channels, over how long
node scripts/import.mjs --since 2026-07-23        # from that day on, five channels a minute
node scripts/import.mjs --status              # where the imports stand (history_imports)
node scripts/import.mjs --resume              # send on the ones that stopped
```

Their labels, vectors and full-text items, when wanted, only theirs, a few first:

```sh
node scripts/replay.mjs --vectorize --imported --limit 50   # vectors and full-text items of 50
node scripts/replay.mjs --vectorize --imported --every 60   # of every imported thread, 50 a minute
node scripts/replay.mjs --relabel --imported --limit 50     # Clef's labels of 50, telling no inbox
node scripts/replay.mjs --relabel --imported --every 60
```

The Entra app needs these Microsoft Graph **application** permissions, with admin consent:
`Team.ReadBasic.All`, `Channel.ReadBasic.All`, `ChannelMessage.Read.All`, `User.Read.All`, and
`TeamsAppInstallation.ReadForTeam.All` (the cron lists each Teams team's installed bots, so replies
can mention them; `/diag/teams?bots=1` runs that once). `TeamMember.Read.All` is optional: with it,
the channel suggestions on the admin pages count the members of a Teams team who never post, and
thread agents can tell who is in a Teams team (`find_member`, and calling out a mention of someone
who is not), and so can the reply box (a suggestion "not in team", and a note after a reply that
mentions them); without it the suggestions go by posts and reactions there, and agents say they cannot
tell. On Slack they use `conversations.members`, which needs the
bot scopes `channels:read` (and `groups:read` for private channels).
`Files.Read.All` (or `Sites.Read.All`) is optional too: with it, the files attached to Teams
messages (SharePoint and OneDrive) are copied into pile 1 and their images shown in the inbox;
without it they stay links. Images pasted into a message (hosted contents) need only
`ChannelMessage.Read.All`.
`AppCatalog.Read.All` is optional as well: with it, Teams bots show their app's icon in the inbox
(Graph beta's `colorIcon` of the app definition installed, `GET /api/bots/icon`, which the cron's
list of installed bots points their picture at); without it they show their initials, unless an
admin gave them a picture in the bot's thread of the admin team's inbox.
For replies and reactions it also needs the **delegated** permissions `ChannelMessage.Send`
(which covers `setReaction`/`unsetReaction` on channel messages and inline images in replies),
`offline_access` and `User.Read`, for files other than images in replies `Files.ReadWrite.All`
(asked for only when someone attaches one), and the web redirect URI
`<origin>/api/auth/teams/callback`; editing one's own
Teams messages from the inbox also needs the delegated `ChannelMessage.ReadWrite` (admin consent,
then `TEAMS_EDITS`); for signing in to
the inbox, the web redirect URI of Access's callback and a client secret held by Access. The
Slack app needs the user scopes `chat:write`, `reactions:write` and `files:write` (the last
asked for only when someone first attaches a file to a reply) and the redirect URLs
`<origin>/api/auth/slack/callback` and Access's callback (Sign in with Slack asks for
`openid`, `email` and `profile` itself); the bot needs `team:read` for the workspace that links to
Slack open in (`team.info`), `reactions:read` for the reaction
events, `emoji:read` for the workspace's custom emoji (`emoji.list`, so a custom `:name:` in a
reply stays as typed and the inbox shows its image; without it Clef replaces no name on Slack
that is not a standard one, since it may be a custom emoji, and the inbox shows them by name),
`files:read` for images and for the files shared in Slack Connect channels (their
events carry only a stub, which opspile looks up with `files.info`), and `files:write` for files
sent as the opspile app. Its Event Subscriptions request URL is
`<origin>/slack/events`.

### Discord

A Discord application reads the servers in `DISCORD_GUILDS` through its bot, read-only:

1. Create the application in the [Developer Portal](https://discord.com/developers/applications);
   under **Bot**, turn on the **Message Content** intent (privileged; no review below 100 servers)
   and turn off **Public Bot** (first set **Installation › Install Link** to *None*). Set the
   bot's token as `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN` (`cf workers secrets update DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN --worker
   helix-opspile-prod --type secret_text --text …`).
2. Add the bot to each server with the permissions *View Channels* and *Read Message History*
   (`https://discord.com/oauth2/authorize?client_id=<app id>&scope=bot&permissions=66560&guild_id=<guild id>`).
   It sees the channels those permissions open to it; private channels need a role.
3. Export `DISCORD_GUILDS` (and for CI, add it as a repository secret), and deploy. The cron
   starts the poller; `POST /diag/discord` polls at once. The first poll reads
   `DISCORD_HISTORY_DAYS` of history.
4. Optionally, `DISCORD_STAFF_ROLES` names the server roles of Adobe people, and with
   `DISCORD_CLIENT_ID` and `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` (OAuth2 redirect
   `<origin>/api/auth/discord/callback`) people connect their Discord account in the inbox.

Discord lets only bots post and react through its API, and opspile acts as the person or not at
all: Discord threads have no reply box, reactions or ✅ in the inbox, only a link to reply in
Discord. Rules match the channel's `name`, and `group_name` is the server's name.

Discord has no webhooks for messages, only a gateway WebSocket, which would hold a Durable Object
in memory (billed by wall-clock time) around the clock. A support server gets a few messages a
day, so opspile polls instead: when nothing happened, a poll is two requests and a few hundred
milliseconds of the object's time. New messages show within a minute, edits, deletions and
reactions within about five.

### Customer portal

Some customers can use neither Slack nor Teams with Adobe. The customer portal is where the
inbox reaches them: they sign in with their work mail address, say what they use of Adobe's,
invite colleagues of the same mail domain and ask for a channel; the admin team (or the managers
of a team that follows what they picked) opens one from its thread in the admin team's inbox, and from then on the
customer writes there and Adobe answers from the inbox. In the inbox the portal is a platform
like Slack, with the source `portal`. See
[docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#customer-portal) for the design.

To set one up:

1. **Access.** Add a self-hosted Access application for the portal's host name, with *One-time
   PIN* as its only login method (and *Instant Auth*, so the sign-in page is the mail address
   field), and one allow policy that includes the mail domains of `PORTAL_DOMAINS` (`Emails
   ending in`). Its audience tag is `PORTAL_ACCESS_AUD`. Everyone who signs in takes an Access
   seat.
2. **The database.** `cf d1 create --name helix-opspile-portal-test`, then
   `cf d1 migrations apply <database id> --dir portal/migrations`.
3. **Deploy it alone**: `PORTAL_DOMAINS=… PORTAL_ACCESS_AUD=… PORTAL_ORIGIN=https://… npm run
   deploy:portal:test`. People can sign in, fill their lobby and ask for a channel.
4. **Connect the two.** Each binds the other's entrypoint, so each must exist first: deploy the
   inbox with this code (it has `PortalSource`), deploy the portal again with
   `PORTAL_OPSPILE_WORKER=helix-opspile-prod`, then deploy the inbox with
   `PORTAL_WORKER=helix-opspile-portal-test` (for CI, the repository secret `PORTAL_WORKER`).
   The portal's next cron run delivers what waited.

### COGS

`/inbox/cogs` (**COGS** in the header, for the admin team) shows what the inbox costs at the
providers' list prices (`src/cogs/prices.js`, with the date they were read and a link to each
price page; the page lists them under **List prices**), filtered by period, service, customer
and team:

- **Flow**: what the period's work used (requests, CPU time, rows, operations, tokens, queries),
  in dollars, per service, and **per message** and **per thread**: divided by the
  messages and the threads started in the period, in what is selected. These estimates (and a
  month at this rate) count from the first whole day every source has counted: the Worker's own
  counts started mid-day, and before them the period's messages were not all costed. A month at
  this rate divides by the time so far, today's hours included. The chart has about as
  many columns whatever the period: an hour each for a week or less (7 days), a day each up to
  45 days (30 days, a month), a week each for longer (90 days).
- **Where the flow goes**: a treemap of the period's flow under the chart, in its card. The
  switch in the card's heading puts both by service (each tile holds its metrics, a model or a
  bucket apiece), by customer (the 10 biggest a colour each, the rest Other), or by team, and is
  in the address (`by=`); a click on a tile picks it, as in the legend and the tables.
- **Stock**: what keeping it costs a month (R2, D1, Vectorize, AI Search, the agents' Durable
  Object storage, the Workers Paid plan, the Cloudflare Access seats): each day's measurement
  averaged over the period.
- **The agents' drafts**: of the drafts the thread agents saved, how many a person sent in the
  period (**accepted**: as they were, or edited first, in the reply box or saved over), discarded,
  or the agent withdrew itself; how many are open now; the replies the agents posted themselves
  (`post_message`); and the flow **per accepted draft** (and how much of it is the agents' model)
  and **per agent reply** (accepted drafts and the agents' own posts). These estimates count from
  the first whole day the drafts were recorded (`draft_events`, migration 0058), as well.
- **Customers** and **teams**, with their messages, threads, flow, cost per message and thread,
  the agents' drafts accepted and the cost per accepted draft, and stock. Work for no customer in particular (the inbox's lists, the cron, the team rooms,
  the buckets) is shared out by each customer's messages (a team's by its customers'), unless
  **Share out common costs** is off; then it is a line of its own, **Shared**.

Costs are list prices at the margin: what a plan includes (10M Workers requests a month, 10,000
neurons a day, …) is listed, not taken off, so a customer's cost is what their work adds, and it
does not depend on who came first in the month.

The one exception is the sign-in. Everyone who signed in to an Access application of the account
(`ACCESS_ACCOUNT_ID`) holds a Zero Trust seat, at $7 a month (Pay-as-you-go), until an admin
removes them or seat expiration does. Every seat is priced: the Free plan's 50 are not taken
off, since past them Pay-as-you-go bills every seat. A seat is its holder's teams' (split between them) when they are a person on ops teams, else
shared. They are the account's seats, the users of its other Access applications too. The
rollup reads them from the Access API once a day, with `ACCESS_API_TOKEN` (which needs *Access:
Audit Logs Read* for it); without the token, or the permission, none are counted.

Where the numbers come from (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "COGS"):

- **The Worker counts** what it uses where it uses it (`src/cogs/meter.js`): every request, queue
  job, cron run and Durable Object gets its bindings counted (D1 rows, R2 operations, queue
  operations, Workers AI tokens per model, Vectorize dimensions, AI Search queries and uploads),
  the agents add their Bedrock tokens (from pi's ledger of the session) and the time their
  browser calls take, and each piece of work names the customer and team it was for (a thread's,
  or its channel's once it is filed). That goes to Analytics Engine (`USAGE`).
- **Cloudflare's analytics** (`ANALYTICS`) give what the Worker cannot see: the Durable Objects'
  requests and duration per object (an agent's are its thread's customer's), the Worker's CPU
  time and log events, Artifacts operations per knowledge base, and what the buckets and indexes
  hold.
- **D1** gives the messages and threads per day, customer and team, and, once a day, its own size,
  the agents' storage and the full-text items.
- **The Access API** gives the account's seats, once a day. The drafts' events (`src/draftevents.js`) are read
  from D1 as they are, not rolled up.

The cron rolls all three up into `cogs_usage` (migration 0051) every 20 minutes, today and
yesterday, and fills in up to three of the last 30 days a run from what Cloudflare's analytics
and D1 still have (the Worker's own counts start at the deploy that brought them). The flow of
the Worker's counts and Cloudflare's analytics is also rolled up by the hour into `cogs_hours`
(migration 0052), kept for eight days, for the chart of a week. Nothing needs
setting up: the dataset is created by its first write, and the Analytics SQL binding needs no
token. A model or metric that is counted but not in the price list is listed under the page,
and costs nothing until it is priced.

Use it for a new version of `@ai-ecoverse/slicc-kernel`, or for a new image, layer or program
(`scripts/computer-images.mjs`): Node's tests cannot run the kernel. `npm run computer:check`
([scripts/dev-computer/check.mjs](scripts/dev-computer/check.mjs)) starts it on a free port and

to the uploads with an exit code and an error, and read them. Add a check for a program you
add. `npm run computer:check -- http://localhost:8788` checks a computer that is running
already. `cf dev` runs the whole Worker with its own local bucket, which
`node scripts/computer-images.mjs --upload --local` fills.

Local workerd does not hold a computer to Cloudflare's limits (128 MB per Dynamic Worker,
module sizes, 10 Dynamic Workers at once), so on a push to any branch but main that changes
the computer, [.github/workflows/computer-stage.yml](.github/workflows/computer-stage.yml)
deploys the same computer to Cloudflare as `helix-opspile-computer-stage` (`wrangler deploy --env stage`, buckets
`helix-opspile-computer-stage-images` and `-files`, made by the workflow), puts the images into
its bucket and runs the same checks there. That Worker is a shell on the internet: it answers
only with the secret `COMPUTER_TOKEN` in `x-opspile-computer-token`, which each run makes and
replaces with an unknown one when it ends. It also gets the run's own `GITHUB_TOKEN` (read-only,
revoked by GitHub when the job ends, deleted from the Worker by the run) as its secret
`GITHUB_TOKEN`, which the computer's gateway puts into git's requests to github.com only, as it
does a team's secrets: git's checks clone as the run, not anonymously from Cloudflare's shared
addresses, which GitHub limits (429). After the checks the run measures what an agent of the
GitHub image costs with the image cache (`--measure`; the workflow's `measure` input tries other
sizes) into its summary. It needs the repository's `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and
`CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, as the deploy does. The check waits for the Worker just deployed to
answer (two minutes at most), and tries a request again, with a bounded backoff, when the
network fails it or it answers 403 (the old token, while the deploy spreads), 429, or a 5xx of
Cloudflare's own (502–504, 520–530); never the Worker's 500, nor a command that ran and failed,
so a broken program still fails the job. What changes the computer is the workflow's `paths`:
itself, `package.json` and its lock (the kernel, the images' programs),
`scripts/computer-images.mjs`, `scripts/dev-computer/`, and every file of `src/` the Worker
imports; `test/computer-stage-paths.test.js` fails when an import is missing from them, or a
file is there that the Worker does not import, and names it.

### Train the team order

Within a status, a team's inbox is ordered by the model in `ui/rank.js`: boosted trees (150
trees of depth 3, some 20 KB) over some fifty features of a thread, computed in the browser from the inbox entry (how long since the
last message, whether it waits on us, a customer's first message nobody at Adobe has answered,
Clef's urgency and resolved, a staff ✅, the kind, unseen by Adobe, reactions, what the last
message asks, …), in log-odds of "the team should turn to this one first". It is a
Bradley–Terry model whose strength is that score, learned from games of three kinds:

- **Judges.** A sample of threads (`pull`), paired in an Elo tournament where Clef says which
  comes first (`duel`); Haiku and Sonnet judge some of the pairs (`llm`), and you judge some
  (`judge`). Each pair is decided by the highest of those who judged it: you, then Sonnet, then
  Haiku, then Clef.
- **Picks.** When someone opens a thread by clicking it in the list, the inbox sends the threads
  on screen then, their places and their features to `POST /api/rank/pick` (table
  `rank_events`; not while viewing as someone else or searching; only in your own views, of a
  thread that exists), with each one's status pile. The cron deletes picks older than 90 days (`RANK_EVENTS_DAYS` in
  `src/rankevents.js`): by then the weekly training has fetched them into `.rank/events.jsonl`. The clicked thread wins over
  each of them: fully over those above it, less over those below it, which may not have been
  looked at. The model orders threads within a pile, so a pick only wins over the others of its
  pile (picks recorded before the piles were sent: over all of them).
- **Replies.** When the same person answers several customers on the same day, the customer
  answered sooner wins over the one answered later, each thread as it was when its customer
  wrote.

Each source is a share of the training (`DEFAULT_SHARES` in `scripts/rank-combo.mjs`: picks
30%, replies 20%, Sonnet 20%, you 15%, Clef 10%, Haiku 5%; a source without games gives its share
to the others), so Clef's thousands of games do not drown out the rest, and what the teams do
weighs more than what anyone says. The steps, each
reading what the ones before wrote into `.rank/` (gitignored; it holds production threads, so
it stays on your machine):

1. `npm run rank -- pull [--n all] [--alerts 0.1]` takes the threads of your teams' inboxes and
   your personal view (for the admin team, every team's, read as one of its members), with their
   messages, from the production API, read-only, with the session `npm run ui:session` copied.
   Alerts are a tenth of the sample. The sample before, and what was judged on it, moves to
   `.rank/<when it was pulled>/`, and is still trained on.
2. `npm run rank -- labels` fetches today's kind of every thread trained on, into
   `.rank/labels.json`. The training uses it instead of the kind a thread had when it was pulled
   or picked: a kind is a thread's subject, and when what the kinds mean changes (`people` and
   `meeting` split off `access` and `other`), the judgements and picks made before stay good.
   Run it after a relabel (`node scripts/replay.mjs --mode prod --relabel`); `weekly` runs it.
3. `npm run rank -- duel [--rounds 12]` plays the tournament with Swiss pairings: Clef reads
   two threads (their messages and what the inbox shows of them) and the team's policy
   (`POLICY` in `scripts/rank-lib.mjs`), and says which comes first, asked both ways round. More
   rounds add to the games played; changing what Clef is shown (`PROMPT_VERSION`) asks again.
   It calls Workers AI with `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` (the Worker's account) and
   `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`, or the token of `wrangler login`.
4. `npm run rank -- llm [--judges haiku,sonnet] [--pairs 200]` has Claude models (on Bedrock,
   with a Bedrock API key in `AWS_BEARER_TOKEN_BEDROCK`, in `AWS_REGION`) judge the same games
   as Clef, shown the same, asked both ways round, with a sentence of why: the pairs you judged,
   then `--pairs` more. `--judges name=<model id>` adds others.
5. `npm run rank -- judge [--port 8796]` shows two threads of Clef's games side by side in the
   browser, and you pick the one the team should turn to first (← →, ↓ for "same", `s` to skip,
   `u` to undo). Then it shows what Clef, the Claude judges (with their reasons), the model and
   the old score said, and how often each agrees with you so far (Clef's also by how sure it
   was, and where asking it the other way round changed its mind). It can show any pair, pairs
   where one or both threads are still pending, the ones Clef was unsure of, or where the model
   and Clef, or the model and the old score, disagree; "Blind" hides the answers. Your answers
   go to `.rank/human.jsonl`. It follows a new sample by itself.
6. `npm run rank -- events` fetches the picks recorded since the last time
   (`GET /api/rank/events`, admin team) into `.rank/events.jsonl`.
   `npm run rank -- experts` does the same for the expert finder's lists and what was done
   with them (`GET /api/experts/events`) into `.rank/expert-events.jsonl`, for a better finder
   to be trained on later; `weekly` runs both.
7. `npm run rank -- train [--model boost|linear] [--trees 150] [--depth 3] [--shares click=0.3,…|counts]
   [--weights …] [--without a,b] [--dry-run]` fits the model on all samples, picks and replies, prints how often the model
   picks the winner of each source's games about a thread it was not trained on (five folds by
   thread: no game with a held-out thread is trained on, whatever its source), next to the hand-made score it replaces (`classicScore` in `ui/inbox.js`), and how
   often each judge agrees with you, and writes the model into `ui/rank.js` (between
   `// MODEL:` and `// END MODEL`). A feature seen in fewer than `--min-support` threads is not
   learned, and the policy's signs (`SIGNS` in `scripts/rank-combo.mjs`: resolved lowers a
   thread; waiting on us, urgency, unseen by Adobe and a first message nobody answered raise
   it) are
   kept: the trees are monotone in them (a split on such a feature must order its leaves so, as
   XGBoost's monotone constraints do); in a linear model (`--model linear`, L2 `--l2 30`) a
   weight the games would turn the other way is held at 0, and the training says which.
   `--without` leaves features out, to see what they are worth.
   Only feature names, thresholds and numbers are committed. A tree is `[feature, threshold,
   left, right]` or a leaf: the browser walks them (some 4 µs a thread), and D1 could as nested
   `CASE` expressions.
8. `npm run rank -- compare [--trees 150] [--depth 3]` puts the linear model next to boosted
   trees and a random forest (`scripts/rank-trees.mjs`, learned from the same games with the same
   pairwise loss), on the same folds: held-out accuracy per source, log loss, size and the time
   to score a thread.
9. `npm run rank -- weekly [--pr]` does it all once a week: a fresh session from the signed-in
   browser, the picks, a new sample, today's kinds, the tournament, the Claude judges on part of
   it, then the training; with `--pr`, the new weights go to a pull request against main (from a worktree of
   it, and only when main computes the same features). A bb automation runs it on Mondays.
10. `npm run rank -- behavior` looks at what the teams do instead: for every thread of the
   sample, whether someone on its team opened it in the inbox and how many read it, and for
   every time a customer wrote, whether and how soon Adobe answered; by kind, urgency, who has
   the next move, size, age, platform, team, and what the message says (a question, alarm words,
   thanks, its length, the time and day), with the odds of an answer within a day from a
   logistic regression, and how well the model, Clef's ratings and the old score pick the threads
   that were opened. It writes `.rank/behavior.md`.

A feature is added in `features()` of `ui/rank.js`, which the browser, the picks and the
training all run; train again after changing one. Picks keep the features they were sent with,
so a new feature is learned from picks only once the inbox computes it. `Rank.explain(entry)`
in the console lists what makes up a thread's score. Without a model (`const MODEL = null`) the
inbox orders by `classicScore`.

### Check the wait estimate

The [customer portal](#customer-portal) tells a customer how long Adobe usually takes to
answer, from the wait model (`src/wait-model.js`, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, "Listening and the next
answer"). Before changing the model, see that it still beats one number for everyone:

```sh
npm run response:waits -- --database <D1 id> [--profile <cf profile>] [--days 28]
```

It reads the last 180 days' conversations from production, read-only (`cf d1 query`), walks
forward a day at a time over the last `--days`, estimating each wait from what was known
before it, and prints counts and errors only, no message and no name. The error is on the log
of the minutes, so 0.7 is about a factor of two.

### Score the product labels

The technologies a thread involves come from three pipelines: Clef's tech questions and the
extraction rules' detections (`labels.tech`, `thread_tech`), and the products Clairic names
(`product` facts, matched to the tech stack by src/products.js `coveredBy`; docs/ARCHITECTURE.md,
"Labels and facts"), which Clairic is no longer asked for: what is left of them scores as it
is. `scripts/truth.mjs` scores them against about 150 channel threads marked by hand. The set is kept in `.truth/`, which is never committed (`.gitignore`): it has thread
titles, customers and the marks.

```sh
npm run truth -- sample --database <D1 id> [--profile <cf profile>] [--n 150] [--seed truth-1]
npm run truth -- score --database <D1 id> [--profile <cf profile>] [--json]
npm run truth -- review --database <D1 id> --origin <inbox origin> [--out file.html]
```

- `sample` draws the threads into `.truth/sample.json`: channel threads of three messages or
  more, filed under a team, not `meeting` or `people`, split over the teams and, within a team,
  over the layer of what Clef and the rules saw (the rarest of a thread's layers, or `none`),
  both by the square root of their size, seeded, at most a tenth from one customer. It keeps
  the population it drew from, so `--from .truth/sample.json` draws the same threads again
  whatever changed in production since; `--before` leaves out the threads that started later.
- `.truth/marks.json` is written by hand: per thread, every entry of the tech stack it involves
  as `uses` (the thread shows the customer uses it) or `about` (the question, the problem or
  the work concerns it, which implies `uses`), the products no entry covers (`missing`, the
  vendor's product name), the parts the customer built (`own`) and a `note`:
  `{ "threads": { "<thread>": { "tech": { "eds": "about" }, "missing": [{ "name": "…" }], "own": [], "note": "" } } }`.
- `score` prints, per pipeline (and their union), the precision and recall of `uses` and of
  `about`, over all marked threads and over those Clef was asked about (a label from before
  migration 0059 has no `labels.tech`), per entry and overall (micro and macro), the hub share
  (the share of the threads that name each entry), and the long-tail recall (of the `missing`
  names, how many each pipeline names in that thread). A pipeline that does not tell `about`
  apart is scored with its `uses`. Ids are read through `tech_moves` where the table exists, so
  the marks' ids from before a catalog move score against labels from after it. `--json` also
  writes `.truth/score-<date>.json`.
- `review` writes `.truth/review.html`, one page with no dependencies: per thread its title
  (a link into the inbox), team, customer and layer, the marks with controls to change them,
  and what Clef and Clairic said. It exports the corrected marks as JSON (a download, else the
  clipboard, else a text box), in the format of `marks.json`.

### Bootstrap the teams' catalogs

The teams' catalogs (`products/` of their knowledge bases, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md "Labels and
facts") start from the tech stack that D1 has: `scripts/catalog-bootstrap.mjs` writes each
technology as a file at the path that will be its id, in the knowledge base of the team that
supports it (`PLACES` in `scripts/catalog-bootstrap-lib.mjs`; the admin team's otherwise). Each
file lists its D1 id in `moved_from`, so the compile rewrites what D1 stores under the old id
once. It reads production and writes nothing there.

```sh
npm run catalog:bootstrap -- --database <D1 id> --out <dir> [--profile <cf profile>]
```

It writes the files under `<dir>/<team>/`, and `<dir>/changes.json` (`[{ repo, path, content }]`,
one file each, as the agents' API takes them). It prints what each technology
becomes and any field the compile would change. Applying them is a write to production: an
admin writes them through the agents' API, one file at a time, so that each write compiles.

### Agents in the browser (WebMCP)

The inbox registers [WebMCP](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/webmcp) tools, so an agent
in the browser (Gemini in Chrome, an extension) can work the inbox through the same UI as the
person signed in, instead of clicking through the DOM. Every tool changes what is on screen
(filters, the open thread, the reply box) and every write goes through the same endpoints as
a click, as that person. A read-only "view as" refuses writes.

| Tool | What |
| --- | --- |
| `get_inbox_state` | Viewer, team, filters, open thread, and counts per filter value |
| `filter_threads` | Set filters (and values to exclude), sort and team |
| `list_threads` | The threads shown, most relevant first |
| `search_threads` | Search on screen, as the search box does; the results, best match first, with the message that matched, and the knowledge bases' entries about it (`knowledge`) |
| `open_thread` | Open a thread (marks it read) and return its latest messages, whole; past 40,000 characters together, the longest are cut to the same length and marked `truncated` |
| `draft_reply` | Put a reply in the reply box for the person to send (in this browser only) |
| `post_draft` | Save a reply as the thread's shared draft, for the team (`base`: the draft it replaces; `sources`: what it rests on, which only the team sees) |
| `post_as_user` | Post a reply as the person |
| `post_as_bot` | Post a reply as the opspile app, where the channel lets the app post |
| `react_to_message` | Add or take off a reaction |
| `resolve_thread` | ✅ on the first message, or take it off again |

Tool output that carries message text or titles is marked `untrustedContentHint`, and
`post_as_user` and `post_as_bot` are marked `consequentialHint`. To try them, turn on
`chrome://flags/#enable-webmcp-testing` and open the inbox (`npm run ui:dev` works) with the
[Model Context Tool Inspector](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/model-context-tool-inspec/gbpdfapgefenggkahomfgkhfehlcenpd)
extension.

### Develop and deploy

**CI** ([.github/workflows/ci.yml](.github/workflows/ci.yml)) runs lint, tests and a build
check on every pull request. A push to `main` applies the D1 migrations and deploys
`helix-opspile-prod`. It authenticates with the repository secrets `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`
(an account token scoped to Workers scripts, R2, Queues, D1, Artifacts and Workers
Observability; it expires after a year) and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, and reads the plain-text vars from the
secrets of the same name. Migrations must be additive, because they run before the new code
is live.

**Pull requests merge through a merge queue** (a ruleset on `main`): `gh pr merge --auto`
puts one in line, and it merges once CI's `test` and the AI review's `ai-review / review` have
passed, on the pull request and again on the queue's merge of it with `main`. Both workflows
run on `merge_group` for that, where the review passes without reviewing again.

To deploy by hand instead: deploys go through the new Cloudflare CLI,
[`cf`](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cf/), which reads `cloudflare.config.ts` (`--mode
prod` or `--mode stage` selects the Worker; the [customer portal](#customer-portal)'s Worker
has its own in `portal/`, which CI builds but does not deploy). `cf deploy` and `cf dev` also build the inbox's
markdown bundle, the knowledge-base editor's text field, the code highlighter's, the stack renderer's and
three.js (`src/ui-markdown.bundle.js`, `src/ui-editor.bundle.js`, `src/ui-highlight.bundle.js`,
`src/ui-fmc.bundle.js` and `src/ui-three.bundle.js`, not committed; `npm run build` by hand). The images of the agents' computers on the SLICC kernel
are not part of the Worker: after the first deploy, and whenever
[scripts/computer-images.mjs](scripts/computer-images.mjs) or a package it uses changes,
`npm run computer:images -- --mode prod` uploads what is new to the `IMAGES` bucket.

```sh
npm install
npm run lint
cf auth create <profile> && cf auth activate <profile>  # once, for the target account
printf 'MS_CLIENT_ID=…\nMS_TENANT_ID=…\nPUBLIC_ORIGIN=https://…\nSTAFF_DOMAINS=…\n' > .env
npm run deploy:prod                                    # or deploy:stage
```

`cf` has no `keep_vars`: the plain-text vars are part of every deploy, so the config reads them
from the environment or `.env` and refuses to deploy without them. Secrets are declared by
name only; `cf deploy` checks that they exist on the Worker and keeps their values. `cf`
cannot set a single secret yet, so use
`npx wrangler secret put SLACK_BOT_TOKEN --name helix-opspile-prod`, or pass
`--secrets-file` to `cf deploy` (required for the first deploy of a new Worker). For
`cf dev`, put the bindings in an uncommitted `.dev.vars`.
